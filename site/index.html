How do people learn what others think?

I study how humans infer other people’s preferences from behavior—and how computational models and AI systems can do the same.

My work brings together cognitive science, decision-making, social cognition, computational modelling, and human–AI interaction. I use behavioral experiments and process-level signals such as response times and eye movements to understand inference as it unfolds.

Research focus

Decisions reveal more than choices.

A choice tells us what someone selected. How quickly they decided and where they looked can reveal how strongly they preferred it and what they attended to. I investigate how people use these cues in social interaction, and whether computational agents can learn from them.
